⚡ Quick Hits — Malnutrition Labs
  • Pre-albumin is the BEST marker for current nutritional status (short half-life ~2 days)
  • Albumin reflects CHRONIC malnutrition — normal 3.5–5.0 g/dL
  • BUN/Creatinine ratio, Iron, Hemoglobin, Hematocrit all checked
  • Electrolytes + Calcium checked (Ca²⁺, Phosphate, Na⁺)
  • Lipid panel (HDL/LDL), A1C, Prealbumin, Osmolality, Amylase/Lipase
Pre-Albumin (Transthyretin)
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 HIGH YIELD
Best marker for CURRENT nutritional status — half-life only ~2 days means it reflects recent changes
  • Normal: 15–35 mg/dL
  • Low pre-albumin = protein malnutrition / inadequate intake
  • Drops quickly with acute stress, infection, inflammation
  • Also called transthyretin
Mnemonic
"Pre-albumin = Present status" — it tells you what is happening NOW
Serum Albumin
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Normal: 3.5–5.0 g/dL
  • Reflects chronic malnutrition — half-life ~20 days
  • Low albumin = hypoalbuminemia → edema (less oncotic pressure)
  • Less responsive to short-term changes than pre-albumin
Mnemonic
"Albumin = Ancient history" — reflects what happened weeks ago
Full Malnutrition Lab Panel
📅 Due
  • Protein markers: Pre-albumin, Albumin, Total Protein
  • Renal/metabolic: BUN, Creatinine, Osmolality
  • Iron studies: Iron, Hemoglobin, Hematocrit
  • Lipids: Cholesterol, HDL/LDL, Lipid Panel
  • Glucose control: A1C, Glucose
  • Electrolytes: Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Phosphate, Mg²⁺
  • Pancreatic: Amylase, Lipase
  • Vitamins: Check for deficiencies if indicated
🟢 Clinical Pearl
Cholesterol used to screen for malnutrition — low total cholesterol indicates poor nutritional intake

  • Antifungal treatment: Miconazole, Nystatin (oral), Diflucan (fluconazole) — topical or oral
  • Scabies = Sarcoptes scabiei mite → treat with Permethrin 5% cream
  • Scabies: wash clothing, keep out of mucus membranes, fire clothing if needed
  • Antifungal: isolation + droplet precautions for some types
  • Permethrin cream: keep away from mucous membranes
Fungal Infection Treatment
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Antifungal creams: Miconazole, clotrimazole (topical)
  • Oral antifungals: Nystatin (swish and swallow), Fluconazole (Diflucan)
  • Isolation: May require droplet precautions
  • Everyday education: Keep skin dry, avoid tight clothing
🔵 Nursing Note
Antifungal creams applied to only affected area — not mucous membranes unless specifically ordered
Scabies — Identification & Treatment
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 Classic Presentation
White ridges/burrows between fingers and on wrists + intense itching = SCABIES until proven otherwise
  • Cause: Sarcoptes scabiei mite burrows under skin
  • Treatment: Permethrin 5% cream — apply from neck down, leave 8–14 hrs, wash off
  • Contact precautions: Required
  • Clothing/bedding: Wash in hot water, dry on high heat — or bag for 72 hrs
  • Keep away from: Mucous membranes, eyes
  • Household contacts: All must be treated simultaneously
Mnemonic
SCABIES: Sarcoptes, Contact precautions, All contacts treat, Burrows in skin, Itching intense, Eliminate mites, Scrub clothes
⚡ Quick Hits — Atrial Fibrillation
  • AFib = chaotic atrial activity → atria quiver instead of pumping correctly
  • Risk: blood clots form in atria → stroke risk → anticoagulate (Warfarin/Heparin)
  • Rate control: Beta-blockers, Digoxin, Ca channel blockers
  • When medications fail → Cardioversion (electrical or pharmacological)
  • Monitor Troponin levels; can use pacemaker, shock, or Atropine
Atrial Fibrillation — Definition & Pathophysiology
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Chaotic, disorganized electrical signals in the atria
  • Atria quiver/fibrillate instead of contracting rhythmically
  • Blood pools in atria → clot formation → embolic stroke risk
  • Ventricular rate is irregularly irregular
  • AV node controls how many impulses get through to ventricles
🔴 Critical Danger
Blood clot in left atrial appendage → travels to brain = STROKE. Must anticoagulate!
Mnemonic
"A-FIB FIBs about rhythm" — the atria lie about being organized; they're chaotic
AFib Treatment Options
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Rate Control: Beta-blockers, Ca²⁺ channel blockers, Digoxin
  • Anticoagulation: Warfarin (monitor INR), Heparin (IV acute)
  • Cardioversion: When meds fail — electrical or pharmacological to restore sinus rhythm
  • Before cardioversion: Must rule out clots (TEE) — minimize clots (Eliquis/Xarelto)
  • Pacemaker: If bradycardia/AV block
  • Atropine: For symptomatic bradycardia
  • Monitor: Troponin levels, ECG, vital signs
🔴 Safety Alert
Hold rate-control meds if systolic BP <90 or HR <50. Always check vitals before giving!
🟢 NCLEX Pearl
Synchronized cardioversion = elective (conscious sedation given). Defibrillation = emergency (no sync). Know the difference!
AFib Nursing Care
📅 Due
  • Continuous cardiac monitoring — watch for rhythm changes
  • Check BP & HR BEFORE giving cardiac medications
  • Bleeding precautions if on anticoagulants
  • Monitor INR for Warfarin (therapeutic 2–3)
  • Healthy diet education — consistent Vitamin K intake with Warfarin
  • Monitor Troponin levels — rule out MI
  • Fall precautions — anticoagulation + possible dizziness
⚡ Quick Hits — Stroke
  • Ischemic (clot/blockage) vs Hemorrhagic (ruptured vessel) — know the difference!
  • TIA = "mini stroke" — resolves within 24 hours; still HIGH risk for full stroke
  • Stroke = interrupted perfusion to brain → brain cells die
  • Risks: HTN, AFib, diabetes, smoking, high cholesterol, obesity, age, diet
  • FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911
Stroke Types
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Ischemic Stroke: Clot/blockage obstructs cerebral artery → most common (~87%)
  • Hemorrhagic Stroke: Ruptured blood vessel → bleeding into brain
  • TIA (Transient Ischemic Attack): "Mini stroke" — symptoms resolve within 24 hrs; NO permanent damage but WARNING sign
🔴 Critical Difference
Ischemic → give tPA (clot buster). Hemorrhagic → tPA CONTRAINDICATED (would worsen bleeding!)
Mnemonic — FAST
Face drooping · Arm weakness · Speech difficulty · Time to call 911
Stroke Risk Factors
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 #1 Risk Factor
Hypertension (HTN) — most modifiable and most common risk factor for stroke
  • AFib, Diabetes, High cholesterol
  • Smoking, Alcohol, Drug use
  • Obesity, Poor diet, Stress
  • Age >55, Immobility
  • Atherosclerosis, Prior TIA or stroke history
Stroke Nursing Interventions
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Airway first — maintain oxygenation, O₂ as needed
  • Neuro checks: Glasgow Coma Scale, pupil response, NIHSS
  • Blood glucose monitoring — hyperglycemia worsens outcomes
  • HOB 30° for ischemic stroke; flat for hemorrhagic initially
  • Swallowing screen BEFORE any oral food/drink/medications
  • Fall precautions — weakness, confusion
  • Antiplatelet therapy (Aspirin) for ischemic
🟢 NCLEX Pearl
"Time is brain" — every minute of stroke = ~1.9 million neurons lost. Act FAST!
⚡ Quick Hits — Post-Angiogram Care
  • Monitor puncture site for BLEEDING — most critical post-procedure concern
  • Check for iodine/contrast/shellfish allergy BEFORE procedure
  • Monitor circulation: cap refill, temperature, pulse distal to site
  • Encourage fluids to flush contrast dye through kidneys
  • Monitor renal function — contrast can cause nephrotoxicity
Post-Angiogram Nursing Care
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 Priority Assessment
Monitor puncture site for BLEEDING — hematoma can form rapidly. Check q15 min × 4, then q30 min × 4
  • Circulation check: Cap refill, temp, color, pulse distal to puncture site
  • Keep extremity STRAIGHT — femoral site: leg straight 6–8 hrs
  • Vitals: Monitor BP, HR, rhythm
  • Increase fluid intake: Flush contrast through kidneys
  • Renal function: Monitor BUN/Creatinine — contrast nephrotoxicity risk
  • Allergy screen: Shellfish/iodine/sedation allergies
  • Monitor for: Infection signs at puncture site, ABC assessment
🟢 Clinical Pearl
Patient should NOT drive for 24 hrs after sedation. Arrange ride home before procedure!
⚡ Quick Hits — Ecchymosis & Petechiae
  • Ecchymosis = bruising — discoloration/redness/purple of the skin
  • Petechiae = tiny red/purple dots from broken capillaries — NOT contagious
  • Petechiae can be red, purple, or brown dots on skin
  • Both are skin conditions, NOT infectious
  • Petechiae seen in low platelet count (thrombocytopenia) — monitor CBC
Ecchymosis (Bruising)
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Definition: Bruising — blood leaks into subcutaneous tissue
  • Color progression: Red → purple/blue → green → yellow (as it heals)
  • Causes: Trauma, anticoagulants, bleeding disorders, falls
  • NOT contagious — no isolation needed
  • Monitor if on anticoagulants — assess extent and progression
Petechiae
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 Clinical Alert
Petechiae that do NOT blanch with pressure = serious — may indicate meningitis, sepsis, or thrombocytopenia
  • Definition: Tiny red/purple/brown dots from broken capillaries
  • Size: Pinpoint (1–2 mm) — scattered on skin
  • NOT contagious — contact isolation NOT required for petechiae alone
  • Do NOT blanch with pressure (unlike red rashes)
  • Causes: Low platelets (thrombocytopenia), infection, trauma, vasculitis
Mnemonic
"Pete's Tiny CHia seeds" — Petechiae = Tiny CHaracteristic dots
⚡ Quick Hits — ACE Inhibitors
  • ACE inhibitors END in "-pril": Lisinopril, Enalapril, Captopril, Benazepril
  • First-dose hypotension: Give at bedtime — monitor BP after first dose!
  • Side effects: Dry cough (ACE cough), Angioedema (STOP immediately), Hyperkalemia
  • Hold if systolic BP <100 mmHg
  • Monitor K⁺ levels — ACE inhibitors raise potassium
ACE Inhibitors — Overview
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Examples: Lisinopril, Enalapril, Captopril, Benazepril (all end in "-pril")
  • MOA: Block angiotensin-converting enzyme → prevent Angiotensin I → II conversion → less vasoconstriction → lower BP
  • Uses: HTN, Heart Failure, Diabetic nephropathy, Post-MI, CKD protection
  • Alternative if ACE intolerable: ARBs (end in "-sartan")
Mnemonic
"ACE = All Could End blood pressure" — and they all END in "-pril"
ACE Inhibitor Side Effects & Nursing
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 STOP DRUG — Angioedema
Swelling of face/lips/tongue/throat = ANGIOEDEMA — STOP ACE inhibitor immediately. Medical emergency!
  • Dry cough: Most common side effect — bradykinin accumulation; switch to ARB
  • First-dose hypotension: Give at bedtime; assist with position changes
  • Orthostatic hypotension: Rise slowly, dangle legs
  • Hyperkalemia: Monitor K⁺ — avoid K⁺ supplements/K⁺-sparing diuretics
  • Hold parameters: Systolic BP <100 mmHg → hold and notify provider
  • Monitor: BP, HR, BUN/Creatinine, K⁺ levels
⭐ MUST KNOW
HOLD ACE inhibitor if systolic BP <100. Monitor K⁺ — they raise potassium!
⚡ Quick Hits — Congestive Heart Failure
  • Left-sided CHF → pulmonary congestion: crackles, SOB, orthopnea, pink frothy sputum
  • Right-sided CHF → systemic/peripheral: JVD, peripheral edema, hepatomegaly, splenomegaly
  • Key: Left = Lungs, Right = Rest of body
  • Low O₂ saturation in left-sided; SOB in both
  • Diuretics (Lasix/furosemide) are first-line treatment
Left-Sided Heart Failure
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 Key Signs
Left heart fails → blood backs up into LUNGS = PULMONARY congestion
  • Crackles (rales) on auscultation — fluid in alveoli
  • Dyspnea/SOB — especially with exertion
  • Orthopnea — can't lie flat (must sleep on pillows)
  • Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea — wakes from sleep gasping
  • Pink frothy sputum — severe pulmonary edema
  • Low O₂ saturation
  • Pulmonary edema
Mnemonic
"Left = Lungs" — Left heart failure backs up into the Lungs
Right-Sided Heart Failure
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 Key Signs
Right heart fails → blood backs up into BODY = SYSTEMIC/PERIPHERAL congestion
  • JVD — Jugular Venous Distension (neck veins bulging)
  • Peripheral edema — pitting edema in legs/ankles/feet
  • Hepatomegaly — enlarged liver
  • Splenomegaly — enlarged spleen
  • Ascites — fluid in abdomen
  • Weight gain from fluid retention
Mnemonic
"Right = Rest of body" — Right heart failure backs up into the Rest of body
⚡ Quick Hits — Pressure Injuries (Stages)
  • Stage 1: Non-blanchable redness — skin INTACT
  • Stage 2: Partial thickness — blister/open wound, no subcutaneous tissue visible
  • Stage 3: Full thickness — fat/subcutaneous tissue visible, no muscle/bone
  • Stage 4: Full thickness — bone/muscle/tendon VISIBLE
  • Reposition every 2 hours to prevent pressure injuries!
Pressure Injury Stages 1–4
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 MUST MEMORIZE — 4 Stages
  • Stage 1: Non-blanchable redness — skin INTACT, warm, firm, painful
  • Stage 2: Partial thickness skin loss — blister or open wound; no subcutaneous tissue
  • Stage 3: Full thickness — subcutaneous fat/tissue visible; no bone/muscle/tendon
  • Stage 4: Full thickness — bone, muscle, or tendon EXPOSED (eschar/slough may be present)
  • Unstageable: Depth unknown — covered by eschar or slough
Mnemonic
"Red Blister Fat Bone" — Stage 1 Red → 2 Blister → 3 Fat → 4 Bone
Pressure Injury Prevention
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Reposition every 2 hours — most important intervention!
  • Use pressure-redistributing mattress/cushion
  • Keep skin clean and dry — moisture leads to breakdown
  • Adequate nutrition and hydration (protein for healing)
  • Braden Scale — assess risk (lower score = higher risk)
  • Heel protectors, offload heels when in bed
  • AVOID donut-shaped cushions — cuts off circulation
🟢 NCLEX Pearl
Braden Scale assesses 6 factors: Sensory perception, Moisture, Activity, Mobility, Nutrition, Friction/Shear. Score ≤18 = at risk
⚡ Quick Hits — Deep Tissue Injury (DTI)
  • DTI = internal bruising — looks like deep bruise or dead blood blister OVER bony prominence
  • Skin is INTACT but damage is underneath
  • Dark colored: blue/purple/black — skin intact!
  • Unknown depth — like a deep bruise forming inside out
  • Turn patient every 2 hours; high risk with immobility
Deep Tissue Injury (DTI)
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Definition: Deep pressure injury — damage to muscle/bone from sustained pressure
  • Appearance: Deep bruise or blood blister — dark blue/purple/black/maroon
  • Skin: INTACT — but damage is underneath ("inside out")
  • Location: Over bony prominences (sacrum, heels, hips)
  • Unknown depth — cannot stage until tissue evolves
  • Risk factors: Immobility, prolonged pressure, bony prominence
🔴 Key Point
DTI looks like internal bruising — appears as if a bruise forms from the inside out. Skin may be INTACT but underneath is damaged!
Mnemonic
"DTI = Deep and Tricky Injury" — deep inside, tricky because skin looks okay
⚡ Quick Hits — Tracheostomy Suctioning
  • Suction ONLY on the way OUT — never insert with suction on
  • Pre-oxygenate BEFORE suctioning (100% O₂)
  • Suction max 10–15 seconds at a time
  • Keep extra tracheostomy set at bedside at all times
  • Always leave obturator (inner cannula guide) taped to head of bed!
Tracheostomy Suctioning Procedure
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 CRITICAL RULE
Apply suction ONLY on the way OUT. NEVER insert the catheter with suction active — causes trauma and hypoxia
  • Step 1: Pre-oxygenate — give 100% O₂ × 30–60 seconds before suctioning
  • Step 2: Insert catheter WITHOUT suction
  • Step 3: Apply suction only on withdrawal — rotate catheter gently
  • Step 4: Maximum 10–15 seconds per suction pass
  • Step 5: Flush with NSS between passes
  • Step 6: Check O₂ sats before and after; monitor for desaturation
  • Always at bedside: Extra tracheostomy set, obturator/inner cannula, 10 mL syringe, suction equipment
🟢 NCLEX Pearl
Patient with trach: NEVER leave obturator at nursing station — tape to head of bed. If trach comes out → insert obturator immediately!
Mnemonic
"Pre-O₂, In dry, OUT suction, 15 sec max"
⚡ Quick Hits — Glaucoma
  • Glaucoma = high intraocular pressure (IOP) → damages optic nerve (CN II)
  • Normal IOP: 10–21 mmHg — above 21 = glaucoma concern
  • Open-angle: gradual, painless peripheral vision loss — most common type
  • Closed-angle: EMERGENCY — severe pain, blurred vision, nausea, halos
  • Vision loss is IRREVERSIBLE — early detection is critical!
Glaucoma — Types & Presentation
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Open-angle (chronic): Gradual, PAINLESS peripheral vision loss; most common (90%). Aqueous humor drains slowly
  • Closed/Narrow-angle (acute): EMERGENCY — severe eye pain, blurred vision, halos around lights, nausea/vomiting, headache. Drain blocked suddenly
🔴 EMERGENCY
Acute closed-angle glaucoma = ophthalmic EMERGENCY. Rapid vision loss within hours if untreated!
🟢 Clinical Pearl
Open-angle: "sneaky" — no pain, patient may not notice until significant vision lost. Check 1–2× per year if family history or age >35
Glaucoma Nursing & Patient Education
📅 Due
  • Eye drops: Apply with punctal occlusion (press inner corner of eye) × 30–60 sec to prevent systemic absorption
  • Teach: Vision loss is IRREVERSIBLE — compliance with drops is critical
  • Avoid: Valsalva maneuver (straining), caffeine, tight collars (increase IOP)
  • Screening: Every 1–2 years after age 35, especially with family history
  • Medications: Beta-blockers (timolol), prostaglandins (latanoprost), alpha-agonists (brimonidine) — all lower IOP
⚡ Quick Hits — Nitrate Medications
  • Used for ANGINA (chest pain) — nitroglycerin (NTG) dilates coronary arteries
  • Headache is a NORMAL side effect — NOT a reason to stop the medication
  • NEVER take with Viagra/Cialis/Levitra (PDE5 inhibitors) — fatal hypotension!
  • Nitro patches must be 12 hrs on / 12 hrs OFF to prevent tolerance
  • Check BP before giving — orthostatic hypotension is a major concern
Nitrate Medications
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION
NEVER give nitrates WITH PDE5 inhibitors (Sildenafil/Viagra, Tadalafil/Cialis, Vardenafil/Levitra) — severe fatal hypotension!
  • Use: Angina (chest pain) — dilates coronary vessels, reduces preload
  • Routes: Sublingual (SL), Topical patch, IV (hospital), Oral long-acting
  • Headache: Normal side effect — from vasodilation; take acetaminophen for relief
  • Nitro patches: Must rotate sites, 12 hrs on / 12 hrs off (prevents tolerance)
  • Orthostatic hypotension: Check BP before and after administration
  • SL NTG protocol: 1 tab q5 min × 3 doses max; if no relief → call 911
Mnemonic
"No Nitrates with Naughty Viagra" — NTG + PDE5 = dangerous drop in BP
⚡ Quick Hits — Gastroenteritis
  • Gastroenteritis = inflammation of stomach and intestines — causes N/V/D
  • NO antibiotics for viral gastroenteritis (most common cause)
  • H. pylori = acute bacterial cause — DOES need antibiotics (triple therapy)
  • Risk: dehydration, electrolyte imbalance — replace fluids aggressively
  • No NSAIDs — can cause GI bleeding/ulcers; use PPI or H₂ blockers
Gastroenteritis — Overview
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Definition: Inflammation of stomach and intestines → N/V/D, cramping
  • Causes: Viral (norovirus, rotavirus — most common), Bacterial (H. pylori, C. diff, Salmonella), Food poisoning, Immuno-suppression
  • Viral: No antibiotics — supportive care only
  • H. pylori: Triple therapy (PPI + 2 antibiotics) — can cause peptic ulcers
🔴 No NSAIDs!
NSAIDs worsen GI irritation and can cause bleeding ulcers. Use acetaminophen for pain instead. Give PPI (omeprazole) or H₂ blocker (famotidine).
  • Complications: Dehydration (most common), Electrolyte imbalance, Ileus
  • Treatment: IV fluids, electrolyte replacement, probiotics, Fluid PO if tolerated
  • Monitor: I&O, electrolytes, skin turgor, mucous membranes
⚡ Quick Hits — Dehydration Risks
  • Dehydration risks: Lasix (furosemide), vomiting/diarrhea, diabetics, elderly
  • C. diff = major cause in hospitalized patients — causes profuse watery diarrhea
  • High sodium (hypernatremia) = sign of severe dehydration
  • Complications: AKI, hypotension, confusion, electrolyte imbalances
  • Replace fluids — isotonic solutions (NS, LR) for moderate-severe dehydration
Dehydration Risk Factors
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
  • Medications: Diuretics (Lasix/furosemide) — most common iatrogenic cause
  • GI losses: Vomiting, Diarrhea (especially C. diff)
  • Endocrine: Diabetes mellitus (osmotic diuresis), Diabetes insipidus
  • Neurological: Confusion, Cognitive impairment — can't recognize/communicate thirst
  • Elderly: Decreased thirst sensation, poor mobility to access fluids
  • Other: Dumping syndrome, C. diff, Malnutrition
🔴 Complications of Dehydration
AKI (acute kidney injury), Hypotension, High Na⁺, Hypotonic solution, Low urine output, Confusion, Electrolyte imbalances
⚡ Quick Hits — Hypokalemia
  • Hypokalemia = K⁺ <3.5 mEq/L — U waves on ECG are CLASSIC finding
  • Signs: muscle weakness, cramps, fatigue, arrhythmias, depression
  • ECG: U waves (after T wave), flat T waves, arrhythmias
  • Treatment: KCl replacement — NEVER IV push! Always dilute and give slowly
  • Lasix (furosemide) is a major cause of hypokalemia
Hypokalemia Signs & Symptoms
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 Critical Value: K⁺ <3.0
Critical hypokalemia → life-threatening arrhythmias. Cardiac monitor required!
  • Normal K⁺: 3.5–5.0 mEq/L. Hypokalemia = <3.5
  • ECG changes: U waves (after T wave), flat/inverted T waves, ST depression
  • Muscle: Weakness, cramps, fatigue, leg cramps, lethargy
  • GI: Nausea, vomiting, constipation, paralytic ileus
  • Cardiac: Arrhythmias, palpitations (most dangerous)
  • Neurological: Depression, confusion, numbness/tingling
🔴 NEVER IV Push K⁺!
Potassium IV must be DILUTED and given SLOWLY (max 10–20 mEq/hr). IV push = CARDIAC ARREST
Mnemonic — Hypokalemia S&S
SUCTION: Skeletal weakness, U waves, Cramps, Tiredness, Ileus, Orthostatic hypotension, Numbness
⚡ Quick Hits — Severe Hyperkalemia Treatment
  • Hyperkalemia = K⁺ >5.0 mEq/L — can cause fatal arrhythmias
  • Treatment: IV Insulin + D50W (drives K⁺ into cells) — FIRST-LINE for severe
  • Kayexalate (sodium polystyrene) — oral/rectal, removes K⁺ from GI tract
  • Calcium Gluconate — protects the HEART; does NOT lower K⁺
  • Sodium bicarbonate — shifts K⁺ into cells in acidotic patients
Severe Hyperkalemia Treatment
🔥 HIGH YIELD📅 Due
🔴 Life-Threatening — ACT FAST
K⁺ >6.0 → peaked T waves, widened QRS, cardiac arrest risk. Treat immediately!
  • 1st: Calcium Gluconate (IV) — cardiac membrane stabilization; protects heart. Does NOT lower K⁺!
  • 2nd: IV Insulin + D50W — drives K⁺ INTO cells (temporary shift). Give together — monitor glucose!
  • 3rd: Kayexalate (sodium polystyrene sulfonate) — PO or rectal; actually removes K⁺ from body via GI tract
  • 4th: Sodium Bicarbonate — in acidosis, shifts K⁺ into cells
  • 5th: Dialysis — definitive removal in renal failure
  • Restrict K⁺ in diet; stop K⁺-sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors
🟢 NCLEX Pearl
Remember: Calcium gluconate = PROTECTS heart (cardioprotective), Insulin/D50W = SHIFTS K⁺ in, Kayexalate = REMOVES K⁺ out of body. Different mechanisms!
Mnemonic — Treatment Order
C-BIG-K: Calcium gluconate, Bicarb, Insulin+Glucose, Kayexalate
